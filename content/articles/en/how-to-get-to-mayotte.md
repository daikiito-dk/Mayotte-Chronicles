Mayotte lies at the north-eastern entrance to the Mozambique Channel, close to Madagascar, and is one of the four islands of the Comoros archipelago. It is part of France, yet getting there takes a little more planning than a trip to Paris or Lyon. There is no direct flight from Asia, the airport sits on a small island separate from the main one, and the entry rules are not the same as in mainland France. This guide takes each step in turn.

**Information checked on November 23, 2026.** Flight schedules and entry rules change, sometimes at short notice. Please confirm the details with your airline and with the official sources listed at the end of this article before you travel.

## Two islands, one airport

Mayotte is made up of two main islands. Grande-Terre is the larger one, and it is home to Mamoudzou, where the Departmental Council has its headquarters. Petite-Terre is much smaller, and it is where you land: the airport, Dzaoudzi–Pamandzi (IATA code DZA), is on Petite-Terre.

This geography shapes the first hour of almost every trip. Unless you are staying on Petite-Terre, you will need to cross a short stretch of sea by boat to reach Grande-Terre. We come back to this crossing below.

## The main air routes

According to the airport, four airlines currently serve Dzaoudzi: Air Austral, Corsair, Kenya Airways and Ewa Air. Between them, they connect Mayotte with mainland France, La Réunion, East Africa and the neighbouring islands.

**Paris.** Air Austral flies non-stop between Dzaoudzi and Paris-Charles de Gaulle. The route opened on June 10, 2016, as the first regular non-stop link between Mayotte and mainland France, and it is operated with a Boeing 787-8. Because the runway at Dzaoudzi is short, the flight sometimes has to make a refuelling stop, for example in Nairobi, when the runway is wet. Corsair also links Mayotte with Paris, but its flights use Paris-Orly rather than Charles de Gaulle.

**La Réunion.** The neighbouring French department is the destination with the most flights in the published schedule. Air Austral and Corsair both fly to Saint-Denis (Roland Garros airport), and the flight takes a little over two hours.

**Nairobi.** Kenya Airways and Air Austral both fly between Dzaoudzi and Nairobi. Kenya Airways also sells connecting itineraries to Dzaoudzi from many cities in its network, including Bangkok and Johannesburg.

**The region.** Ewa Air, a subsidiary of the Air Austral group, flies to several cities in Madagascar, including Majunga, Nosy Be, Diego-Suarez and Antananarivo, as well as to Moroni in the Comoros.

Frequencies change with the seasons. At the time of checking, the published schedules showed La Réunion with the most weekly flights, followed by Nairobi and Paris-Charles de Gaulle.

## Example routes from Japan

There is no direct flight between Japan and Mayotte, so the journey involves at least two flights and usually two connections. Here are three patterns. They are examples rather than recommendations; prices and connection times vary widely.

1. **Via Paris.** Air France, ANA and Japan Airlines fly non-stop between Tokyo Haneda and Paris-Charles de Gaulle. From Charles de Gaulle, Air Austral continues non-stop to Dzaoudzi. If you plan to fly with Corsair instead, remember that its flights leave from Orly, another airport in the Paris region, and allow plenty of time for the transfer.
2. **Via Paris and La Réunion.** Corsair, among others, flies between Paris and Saint-Denis on La Réunion, and the regional flight from Saint-Denis to Dzaoudzi takes a little over two hours. This route makes it easy to combine the two islands in one trip.
3. **Via Bangkok and Nairobi.** Several airlines fly non-stop between Tokyo Narita and Bangkok, and Kenya Airways flies non-stop between Bangkok and Nairobi. From Nairobi, Kenya Airways or Air Austral continue to Dzaoudzi.

If you connect in Paris, you enter the Schengen area before leaving it again for Mayotte. Check the rules that apply to your stopover as well as those for Mayotte itself.

## From the airport to Grande-Terre: the barge

The link between the two islands is the barge, a ferry service that carries both foot passengers and vehicles. On Petite-Terre, barges leave from Dzaoudzi, at the Issoufali and Ballou quays. On Grande-Terre, they arrive in Mamoudzou, at the main ferry terminal and at the Colas quay.

During the day, departures run roughly every half hour. On weekdays the first boat from the Issoufali quay leaves at 5:30 a.m., and in the evening the service becomes roughly hourly. It continues until around half past midnight on most nights, and later on Friday and Saturday nights and on the eve of public holidays. On Sundays and public holidays, the first departure from Issoufali is at 6:00 a.m., and the other quays have about one departure per hour.

Fares are modest and are charged in one direction only. According to the airport, a foot passenger pays €0.75 from Mamoudzou to Dzaoudzi, while the crossing from Dzaoudzi to Mamoudzou is free. A car costs €15 in the Mamoudzou to Dzaoudzi direction and nothing the other way. In other words, the crossing from the airport side to Grande-Terre costs you nothing.

To get from the terminal to the barge, taxis labelled "Aéroport" wait in a dedicated area and charge a single fare per vehicle for up to four passengers. The airport lists this fare as €5.60 by day and €8.40 at night, on Sundays and on public holidays. These figures come from a prefectoral order dating from 2014, so they may have changed. The airport website also lists car rental and a private water-taxi service between the two islands.

## Entry requirements: France, but outside Schengen

Mayotte is a French department and, since January 1, 2014, an outermost region of the European Union. It is not, however, part of the Schengen area. France-Visas, the French government's official visa website, states that none of France's territories outside Europe belong to the Schengen area, and it lists Mayotte among them.

This has several practical consequences.

- **There is a border check on arrival.** The airport and the port of Dzaoudzi are listed as controlled border crossing points in the French order that sets the rules for entering Mayotte. Even if you fly in from Paris, have your passport or identity card ready.
- **EU, EEA and Swiss citizens** can enter and stay without any special formalities, according to service-public.fr, the French administration's official information site. A valid passport or national identity card is enough.
- **Japanese citizens** do not need a visa for stays of up to 90 days within any 180-day period. Mayotte has its own list of visa-exempt nationalities, set by an order of February 4, 2015 (in the version in force since June 19, 2025), and Japan is on that list. The exemption does not apply to a stay for paid work that requires a work permit, if you cannot present that permit at the border.
- **Passport conditions.** For non-European visitors, service-public.fr states that the passport must be valid for at least three months after the date you plan to leave, contain two blank pages, and have been issued within the previous ten years. Air Austral also asks foreign nationals to hold a return or onward ticket.
- **If you need a visa,** a Schengen visa is not enough on its own. You need a visa issued for the specific overseas territory you are visiting; Air Austral notes that a Schengen visa can only be used if the visa sticker mentions "Valid for Mayotte". Conversely, a visa issued only for an overseas territory does not allow you to enter the Schengen area.

The online visa assistant on France-Visas is the simplest way to confirm what applies to your own nationality and situation.

## Before you go

On December 14, 2024, Cyclone Chido struck Mayotte, and Tropical Storm Dikeledi followed in January 2025. According to the French Ministry of the Interior, the port, the roads, the airport, schools, the hospital and public services in general were severely damaged. Reconstruction was still under way in 2026. The ministry's list of ongoing projects includes a new airport at Bouyouni on Grande-Terre and a desalination plant intended to help end the "tours d'eau", the system of supplying water to different sectors in rotation.

For visitors, this simply means it is worth checking recent news, confirming your accommodation directly, and following announcements from your airline before and during your stay.

Getting to Mayotte takes time. But once the barge pulls away from Dzaoudzi and Grande-Terre comes into view, the long journey is almost over.
